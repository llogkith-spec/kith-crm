Part A

Before you price

Read this part once, properly. The rest of the document you look things up in.

00

Who is the customer?

Answer this before you touch anything else. There are six customer types and they do not share a rate card. The same 1.5-hour job is £210 to one of them and £66 to another. Getting this wrong is the single most expensive mistake on the desk, in both directions.

CustomerHow you recognise itWhat prices it
Retail / B2CA member of the public. The default.Ladder, 2.0 hr threshold, two tiers
Trade / B2BA company flagged trade in the CRM.Ladder, 1.5 hr threshold, three tiers
AA SMARTSmart-repair work routed by the AA.£55/hr flat, parts 15% — §33

AA PGCAn AA warranty claim.£90/hr flat, parts NOT SET — §35

The two ways this goes wrong

Pricing an AA job at retail means an invoice the AA will not pay. Pricing a retail customer off an AA card earns roughly a third of what the job is worth, and nobody notices until the month is counted. The intake form records the customer type — read it.

01

Who is allowed to price

Only estimators price jobs. Service Advisers take the customer in and open the CRM record; they do not quote. If you do not hold the estimator role, the job goes to Logan.

You do not need to know anything about cars to do this job. That is deliberate. The estimator's seat follows a process accurately; it does not make mechanical judgement calls. Everywhere a judgement call is needed, this document tells you to stop and ask.

02

Safety triage belongs to the adviser

Safety-keyword detection happens at intake, not at your desk. If a customer mentions a warning light, grinding, smoke, a no-start, loose steering, a burning smell or overheating, the Service Adviser books a diagnostic first. Only after the diagnostic does the job reach an estimator.

Never quote off a description

Quote off the diagnostic findings in the CRM. If the record shows a symptom and no diagnostic, kick it back to the Service Adviser — do not price around it, and never tell a customer a car is safe to drive.

03

The five never-break rules

  1. Establish the customer type first. §00. Everything downstream depends on it.
  2. Never quote raw SRT to a KITH customer. Hours go through the ladder in §06. AA customers are the exception — they are charged at raw SRT and never take a multiplier.
  3. Never round hours. Only round money. A 20-minute inspection is 20 ÷ 60, not 0.333. And never round an SRT to tidy it — nudging 1.9 up to 2.0 takes about £70 off the quote.
  4. Never price a part off a supplier's website. Our account price is different. Phone them (§16).
  5. Never guess. If a price is not in this document, it does not exist yet. Escalate (§44). A late quote is recoverable; a wrong one is not.
04

The eight steps

  1. Establish the customer type. §00.
  2. Check there is a diagnostic. §02. No diagnostic on a symptom job means it goes back.
  3. Identify the vehicle. Registration, make, model, engine, fuel, year. Check the reg on the TechMan screen matches the car.
  4. Check the ramp-exempt tag. §07. Set by Logan in the CRM, never by you.
  5. Break the request into jobs. "Service and brakes" is two jobs. Brakes may be two more.
  6. Get the SRT for each job and classify it. Tier (§05) and category (§08). Category matters as much as tier — it decides whether the ladder applies at all.
  7. Price labour, then parts. Ladder (§06), pair rule (§09), minimums (§10). Then phone the supplier, raise a PO, and apply §13 — remembering fluids and tyres skip it.
  8. Check before it goes. Run §43. Anything on the list in §44 goes to Logan, not the customer.

TechMan gives you book time. We do not charge book time. Take the SRT for one line, find the band, multiply. That gives charged hours.

The quirk we accept

There is no floor. So a 1.99 hr line bills 3.98 charged hours and a 2.00 hr line bills 3.00 — the bigger job is cheaper. At TOP that is a £68.60 step down.

07

Ramp exemption

When a vehicle cannot go on the ramp because of its height or weight, the multipliers lift:

BandNormalRamp-exempt
Lower×2×2.5
Upper×1.5×1.75
They replace, they do not stack

×2.5 is used instead of ×2, not on top of it. A 1.2 hr ramp-exempt job is 1.2 × 2.5 = 3.0 charged hours. It is never 1.2 × 2 × 2.5.

The estimator does not decide ramp exemption. Logan tags the job in the CRM and you apply the uplift. It applies to mechanical and bodywork labour, and to the always-×2 categories (which go to ×2.5). Trade and retail get the same uplift. It does not apply to AA work.

12

What counts as a line

One SRT is one line, and each line picks its own multiplier from its own hours.

The single exception: a service pools its SRTs — oil SRT plus filter changes plus inspection time — into one combined line with one multiplier taken from the combined total. Nothing else pools. Brakes, clutch and suspension are each their own line.

The parts ladder

Applied per part, on that part's own net unit cost. Quantity never bands a part up. Same ladder for retail and trade.

£0.01 – £10.00×2.0—
£10.01 – £20.00×1.8£20.00

£30.01 – £40.00×1.4£48.00

The number itself stays in the lower band: £10.00 takes ×2.0, and you must reach £10.01 to drop to ×1.8.

What the minimum sell is for

Without it the bands invert — a £30.01 part would sell for £42.01 while a £30.00 part sells for £48.00. The minimum sell holds the price flat across the band edge until the multiplier catches up. It applies per part, like the multiplier.

One consequence worth knowing: in the top band, everything netting between £40.01 and £43.07 sells at exactly £56.00.

Four spark plugs at £9.00 net each Each plug £9.00 → £0.01–10 band → ×2.0 → £18.00 Four of them = £72.00 NOT £36.00 as a line total → ×1.4 → £50.40. Quantity does not band up.

Sourcing & grade

  1. Phone the supplier. Not the website. Account prices differ from public prices, and quoting off a website is how a job loses money before it starts.
  2. Say "KITH Cars", give the registration, state the part.
  3. Ask for price and ETA. Both.
  4. Fill out a PO.
  5. If nobody has the part, tell Logan. Do not substitute on your own judgement.

Choose on cheapest + best quality + ETA together. No supplier is the default first call.

17

Customer-supplied parts

Default answer: no. KITH does not fit customer-supplied parts, and never accepts a customer-supplied engine.

Exception: on high-value parts (around £1,000+), ask Logan every time. If approved, the customer signs a disclaimer at intake — no warranty on the part, no warranty on the labour if the part fails, no liability.

If mid-job we find the customer's part is wrong or unfit, we refuse mid-way and charge half the labour for what has been done.

Part G

Trade, VAT & commercials

The customer-type and money-handling rules that sit around every quote.

38

Trade unlock

Trade status unlocks when a company brings 4 different cars to KITH within a 30-day window. Tracked in the CRM by company name and registration numbers.

  • The first four cars are always retail. No retrospective refund.
  • Trade pricing starts at vehicle five.
  • Once unlocked it is permanent. No rolling monthly requirement — they stay trade even after a quiet spell.
  • Related businesses run by the same person can share trade status.

43

TechMan checklist

TechMan generates the estimate from SRTs. It does not apply this SOP. Correct all of this before anything is sent:

  1. Customer type checked. Retail has no MED tier. AA work has no multiplier at all.
  2. Charged hours entered, not raw SRT. TechMan hands you book time.
  3. SRT not rounded. 1.9 typed as 2.0 costs about £70.
  4. Ramp-exempt flag checked against the CRM tag. ×2.5 / ×1.75 replace, never stack.
  5. Always-×2 categories classified correctly — suspension, bearings, DPF, track rods.
  6. Pair rule used only for the closed three.
  7. Parts on the ladder, banded per part. Quantity does not band up.
  8. Minimum sell applied per part.
  9. Fluids, consumables and tyres kept out of the ladder.
  10. MOT off the VAT — its own line, "VAT not applicable".
  11. Inspection time as minutes, not 0.208 / 0.333.
  12. No rounding of hours anywhere; money rounded only at the end.
44

Escalate to Logan

No pound threshold triggers escalation — Logan checks every quote anyway. But you must hard-stop on all of these:

  • Any customer-supplied part
  • Any part costing over £2,000 net
  • Any pairing not on the closed list of three
  • Any SRT not found in HaynesPro after also calling the main dealer
  • Any vehicle above 8 seats
  • Any split rim alloy
  • Any expensive-make paint
  • Any recovery beyond 10 miles
  • Any accident or insurance work
  • Any sill rot welding
  • Anything in the open items register (§51)
  • Anything you are not confident about — that is a sufficient reason on its own
The cost of asking versus the cost of guessing

An escalation costs a few hours of delay. A wrong quote costs the margin on the job, and it is very hard to raise a price once a customer has seen a number. Escalating is never the wrong call.

45

Extra work mid-job

Work found after the car is in goes back through the CRM as a revised quote, and the customer approves it in writing before the work happens. It is not added to the invoice at the end and it is not agreed verbally in the workshop.

Price the extra work exactly as if it were a fresh quote — its own line, its own band on the ladder. It inherits nothing from the original job, because the multiplier is per line (§12).

46

The estimator app

The app applies every rule in Parts B to F automatically and shows its working line by line, so you can check the arithmetic against this document. It also blocks the send when a §44 hard stop is present.

What the app will not do: decide the customer type, decide the ramp-exempt flag, read an SRT out of TechMan for you, or judge whether a pairing qualifies. Those stay with you and with Logan.

The AI never touches the arithmetic

The AI layer helps break a description into jobs, tells you where to click in TechMan, and sanity-checks a finished quote. Every number is produced by the deterministic engine, which is verified against this document by 141 automated checks. If the app and this SOP ever disagree, that is a bug — flag it to Logan the same day.

Glossary

SRT
Standard Repair Time. The book hours HaynesPro or Autodata give. What you look up — not what you charge, unless the customer is AA.
Charged hours
SRT after the multiplier. What goes on the quote.
Customer type
One of six. Decided at intake, recorded in the CRM, and the first thing you check.
The ladder
Two of them. The labour ladder (§06) turns SRT into charged hours. The parts ladder (§13) turns net cost into a selling price.
Threshold
Where the multiplier steps from ×2 to ×1.5. Retail 2.0 hr, trade 1.5 hr. Landing exactly on it takes ×1.5.
Always-×2
The four categories in §08 that never step down.
Ramp exemption
A CRM tag set by Logan for vehicles that cannot go on the ramp. Multipliers become ×2.5 / ×1.75, replacing ×2 / ×1.5.
Pair rule
The closed list of three in §09. The second item bills at raw SRT.
Line
One SRT or one part. Each picks its own multiplier or band. Pooling happens only inside a service.
Minimum sell
The floor under each parts band that stops the ladder inverting at a band edge. Applied per part.
Net cost
What KITH pays a supplier. Never what the customer sees.
PO
Purchase order. Raised for every part, after a phone quote.
TechMan
The garage management system. Generates estimates and invoices; does not apply this SOP.
Estimate / quote
The same thing.
